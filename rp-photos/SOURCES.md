# rp-photos — source and licence record

**These images are AI-generated.** Confirmed by the owner, 2026-08-17. That
answers the question this file was opened to answer, and it is recorded here
because nothing in the files themselves says so — they were stripped of all
metadata before they were committed (no EXIF, no XMP, no C2PA; checked file by
file). The `Exif` byte sequence in `maya.jpg` is a false positive: those bytes
are JPEG Huffman tables, not tags.

## What that settles, and what it does not

**Settled — and this is the good news.** No model releases, no likeness rights,
no photographer attribution. AI-generated faces are lower risk in a published
app than stock photographs of real people, because there is no real person with
a claim. The folder README's warning about Google Images does not apply.

**Not settled: whether the commercial rights are actually yours.** That depends
entirely on WHICH tool produced them and on WHICH PLAN. The generators differ,
and several grant commercial use only on a paid tier — a free-tier output can
carry restrictions that a paid one does not. So the outstanding fact is not
"where did these come from" but:

> **Which tool generated these, and were you on a plan that grants commercial
> use at the time?**

Record the answer below. If any of them came from a free tier whose terms do not
grant commercial redistribution, regenerate those on a plan that does, rather
than leaving it to chance in a published app.

**A residual worth one look.** Generators occasionally produce a face that
resembles a real person closely enough to matter. The five cast portraits carry
names and job titles in the app, which is the combination most likely to be
mistaken for a real endorsement. A reverse-image search on those five is cheap
insurance and probably finds nothing.
