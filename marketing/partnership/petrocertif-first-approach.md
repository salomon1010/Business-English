# First approach — Petrocertif Construction Academy (jfn-academy.com)

**INTERNAL.** Send the PDF, not this file.

---

## Read this before you send anything

The cold-outreach template in the target list is **not right for this one**, and
using it would be a mistake. Three differences:

1. **He is a friend.** A formal five-sentence cold email to a friend reads as
   distant, or worse, as if you are handling him. Write to him the way you
   normally do, and let the *attachment* be the formal object.
2. **They already promise your app in public.** Their course offer says
   *"Enhance your professional English skills … and a shadowing approach via our
   LomoneC App: app.lomonec.com."* You are not asking them to adopt something
   new. You are asking to make a promise they have already published real, and
   evidenced.
3. **The document's real reader is not him.** He is one partner among several.
   The proposal exists so he can forward it and have the others take it
   seriously without him having to explain you. Write for the partner who has
   never met you.

**Say out loud that you are also practising.** You want his opinion on the
approach itself, and hiding that would be the one thing that could actually
damage a friendship. It also happens to be flattering: you are asking him to
review your work before you take it to strangers.

## The three questions to ask on the call

Not "what do you think" — that gets politeness. Ask these:

1. **"If I were a stranger, what would stop you saying yes?"** The real
   objection, from someone who knows the sector.
2. **"Which page would you forward to your partners, and which would you
   delete?"** Tells you what carries weight and what is decoration.
3. **"What would make this obviously worth paying for after the pilot?"** He
   knows what his academy would actually buy. This is your pricing research and
   it costs you nothing.

Write the answers down the same day.

## One thing to raise with him, gently, before the cohort starts

Their published line says *"certification in written and spoken English."*

BE Mastery does not do that. It is speaking-first, it does not assess written
English at all, and the certificate it issues is a **completion certificate for
the 84-session programme** — not a recognised proficiency qualification like
IELTS or a CEFR level.

If a learner enrols expecting a written-English certification and does not get
one, the complaint lands on **his** academy, not on you. Raising it now costs
one sentence and buys enormous trust. Suggested replacement wording:

> *Strengthen your spoken professional English through guided daily practice and
> workplace speaking simulations, with a record of the speaking work you have
> completed — via the BE Mastery app by Lomonec.*

This is also why Section 06 of the proposal states it in writing. Leave that
section exactly as it is.

## Scope note

The pilot is deliberately limited to **boilermaking, industrial piping and
welding production supervision**, because those are the three trades BE Mastery
carries today. Their other six disciplines — electrical, civil, instrumentation,
renewable, metal structures, storage tanks — are not built yet. Offering all
nine would be a promise you cannot keep in four weeks, and he would find out.

The proposal turns that limit into the upside: the pilot decides which
discipline gets built next, and Petrocertif chooses it.

---

## After the pilot

Whatever the numbers say, write it up. A cohort that half-completes is still
Case Study #1, and an honest write-up of a mediocre result is more persuasive to
a training director than a glowing one with no method. Get his written
permission to name Petrocertif before you cite them to anyone — that is the
caution from the target list, and it applies to friends too.
